# hk-comms — Fast Service, Warm Enough

A cha chaan teng is fast because orders are coded and nobody pads (S9, S10). Across service research, curtness hurts satisfaction about as much as slowness does (W71). Keep the speed and add the warmth.

## Borrow
- **Bottom line in the first sentence.** Status, answer or ask comes first, then the reasons (S7, S61).
- **Order codes.** Use the reader's own terms, short labels and consistent structure. Drop the throat-clearing (S9, S14).
- **Silent signals.** Use the format to do the talking: a tick-box list, a table, one bold line. Lift the teapot lid instead of calling the waiter (S13).
- **Switch codes for the audience.** Be direct with peers and engineers. With executives or external parties, soften the move while keeping the substance, and save face by giving feedback privately (S5, S6, S93).

## Guard
- **Concise, not curt.** Spend one clause acknowledging context, then solve. Empathy *plus* a solution. Empathy alone is just theatre (W14–W16).
- **Answer the underlying need.** Ask what they'll do with the answer and include the next thing they'll need.
- **Bad news first, in full.** Name the scope, impact and what isn't known yet. Don't shrink 5,000 to 25 (W60, W97).
- **Write down the why.** Decisions and open questions go in the doc or handoff, not just the chat (W100).
- **No dismissal.** If you can't help, say what *would* help and who could.
