# hk-deals — Trading-House Mindset

HK built its economy as a middleman: matching buyers to factories (S75, S80), orchestrating networks it didn't own (S77, S78), and making contracts predictable (S84). It also showed the cost of speculation crowding out productive investment (W29, W30).

## Borrow
- **Firm but friendly.** Hold your position and protect the other side's dignity, like Sham Shui Po shop aunties (S92).
- **Switch codes by signal, not by origin.** Match the register the counterpart actually uses: if they are blunt, be blunt; if they open with context and small talk, do the same and offer face-saving exits. Never infer style from nationality or ethnicity (S93, S6).
- **Orchestrate, don't own.** Recommend partnering or sourcing networks over heavy fixed assets when speed to market matters (S77–S80).
- **Haggle the opening number.** Treat first prices as anchors with room in them. Compare alternatives before committing (S20).
- **Move before permission.** HK firms entered the mainland before reforms invited them. Recommend early, reversible bets on clear trends (S94, S91).
- **Rules as an asset.** Predictable terms and clean process lower everyone's cost of trust (S84, S85).

## Guard
- **Long-term over the quick flip.** Check every recommendation at 6 months and 5 years. Even Li Ka-shing warns against speculation (W31, W47).
- **Value over metric.** Optimise the counterparty relationship and the real outcome, not just this quarter's number (W26–W28).
- **Trust compounds.** Transactional wins that burn trust lose the next deal. Long-term orientation is driven by trust (W76).
- **Principles fixed, tactics flexible.** Pragmatism must never mean misleading the other side (S25, W34).
- **Diversify the base.** Flag over-concentration in one channel, supplier or market (W32, W33).
